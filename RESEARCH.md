## Observations

**Parker Dewey – Career Launchers**
- Value for students fits in three short promises: always paid, flexible around classes, and simple because the platform handles the paperwork.
- A four-step "how it works": create a profile, browse (details, timeline, pay), apply, complete the assignment.
- The page leaves out scope, actual pay figures and how candidates are chosen. All of that only appears once you browse listings.
- CTAs are short imperatives that stress low effort ("Start Here", "Create an account", sign-up in under 10 minutes).

**Parker Dewey – FAQ**
- Accordion grouped by audience (basics, employers, students, institutions), with keyboard instructions.
- Projects are 10–40 hours and due one week to one month after kickoff. Larger work is split into smaller assignments.
- Fixed fee shown to the student before applying, with no bidding. Payment is held until the client approves the work.
- Listings show deliverable, time commitment, due date, industry and pay. Applicants answer project-specific questions, and companies pick from profiles and answers.
- On completion the client rates timeliness, communication, quality, presentation and skill level. The page does not mention portfolios or references.

**Riipen – home**
- Audience cards (learners, educators, employers) with one value line each, then tabs with longer benefit lists.
- Employer value: get projects done, early recruiting, fresh perspectives. Learner value: paid, remote work that builds job skills.
- No visible explanation of how projects are scoped, reviewed or evidenced. Uses partner logos and impact numbers.

## How this shaped Mihmah

- **Value propositions:** the landing page has two short audience panels (students / companies), each with 3–4 benefits and its own CTA, like Riipen's audience cards. Unlike the references, there are no partner logos, statistics or testimonials, because Mihmah has none yet.
- **Scope, pay, deadline up front:** task cards and task details always show a fixed budget in JOD, an estimated duration, a delivery date, deliverables and required skills, following the Parker Dewey FAQ's listing fields. The budget is marked as a demo amount with no real transaction.
- **Short, split work:** seeded tasks run 4–21 days with 2–3 milestones each, mirroring the advice to split large projects.
- **Applications:** students send a proposal, a portfolio link and an expected delivery time, which play the role of the FAQ's "project-specific questions". Companies choose from the profile plus proposal, with the task's skills highlighted against the student's.
- **Milestones and communication:** the references don't show these publicly, so the project workspace adds milestones, saved messages, an activity log and an explicit submit → review → revise → approve loop.
- **Feedback and portfolio:** Parker Dewey's completion rating informed the approval step (star rating + written feedback). Since neither site describes portfolios, completed projects and their feedback go straight into the student's portfolio, and a printable completion summary is offered, marked as a prototype and not a certificate.
- **FAQ:** native `<details>` accordion, keyboard-accessible by default, answering the questions the references raise (duration, pay, selection, revisions).
